Privacy Policy

Effective Date: November 21, 2026  ·  OrionPulseNet

Overview

OrionPulseNet is developed by Mrinal Singh Raja (MSRX). We take privacy seriously and are committed to protecting your personal information. This policy explains what data we collect, how we use it, and your rights.

Information We Collect

MSRX PulseNet (formerly OrionPulseNet) collects no personal data. We run no servers for the app, and it has no account, analytics, advertising or tracking. Readings such as reply time, traffic and your history of outages stay on your Mac.

To measure your connection, the app talks directly to public services from your Mac: speed-test traffic goes to Cloudflare (speed.cloudflare.com), and connection checks go to 1.1.1.1, 8.8.8.8 and public websites. Those services can see your IP address, as any website you visit can. The Sites screen contacts only the popular sites listed there, plus any site you add yourself. The Network tools contact only the site, host or address you type, and for DNS lookups they also ask the public DNS resolvers Cloudflare (1.1.1.1), Google (8.8.8.8), Quad9 (9.9.9.9) and OpenDNS (208.67.222.222) about the name you typed. Those sites and resolvers can see your IP address and the name you asked about, as with any lookup. We receive none of it. Mac load, Wi-Fi security and Wi-Fi signal are read from your Mac and are never sent anywhere. The Wi-Fi name is read only if you tap Allow on the Location prompt, and it never leaves your Mac. The Ask feature uses Apple Intelligence on your Mac only.

More recent features work the same way: nothing is collected by us, and everything they save stays on your Mac.

  • Devices. When you tap Scan, the app asks your Mac’s local network which devices answer, and shows their address, name and the services they offer. macOS asks your permission first (Local Network). The list, and a note of which devices you have seen before, are saved only on your Mac. They are never sent anywhere.
  • Wi-Fi. Wi-Fi security, nearby networks, channel advice and room-by-room signal are read from your Mac. Nearby network names are shown only if you tap Allow on the Location prompt. They are used on screen and never leave your Mac. Your location itself is not stored or sent.
  • Network checks. The lag-under-load, DNS speed, cloud and game latency, IPv6 and DNSSEC checks, and the safety check connect from your Mac to public services only: Cloudflare, Google, Quad9 and OpenDNS resolvers, Apple and Google connection-test pages, and Amazon’s public cloud addresses for the region latency test. Those services can see your IP address, as with any connection. We receive none of it.
  • Tune-up and DNS profile. Tune-up only reads and recommends. It changes no setting on its own. If you choose to switch DNS, the app saves a configuration profile file where you tell it to. It takes effect only if you open it and approve it yourself in System Settings.
  • Notifications. Alerts (outage, slow connection, data cap, weekly summary) are created on your Mac and shown by macOS. They go through no server of ours. The app asks permission only when you turn an alert on.
  • Exports and sharing. The provider report, share card and CSV or JSON export are made on your Mac and saved only where you choose. The report holds no name, address or location. What you do with a saved file is up to you.
  • Status, history and journal. The Status page, History, busy-hours heatmap, outage replay, data usage, plan details and notes you write are kept as small files in the app’s private folder on your Mac.
  • Widget and menu bar. The desktop widget reads a small private file that the app shares with it on your Mac. It holds readings only, with no Wi-Fi name or address.

Data Storage

MSRX PulseNet keeps all readings, history, device lists, plan details and journal notes on your device. Deleting the app removes them. We do not sell, rent, or share your personal information with anyone.

Third-Party Services

We use no third-party SDKs. Apple distributes the app through the Mac App Store, and Cloudflare serves the speed-test files, as described above.

Children's Privacy

Our apps are not directed to children under 13. We do not knowingly collect personal information from children.
